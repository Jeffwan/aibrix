## A.2 External FAQ (customers)

**Q: What is AIBrix Batch?**
An open-source, self-hosted, OpenAI-Batch-compatible service. Same `/v1/files` + `/v1/batches` API and JSONL format, 24-hour completion window — but it runs on *your* Kubernetes cluster and your GPUs, and it actively lowers the cost of each batch token instead of charging a fixed discount.

**Q: How is it cheaper than OpenAI/Anthropic Batch's 50% off?**
Their 50% is a *pricing* concession on a black box. AIBrix lowers the *actual cost basis* three ways, which compound: (1) higher throughput-per-GPU via batch-aware scheduling; (2) **harvesting idle capacity** on your existing online fleet, where a batch token's marginal cost is near zero; (3) (later releases) cheaper hardware tiers and cross-region spot. Your effective price depends on how much idle capacity you have — see our cost calculator. The more your online fleet is over-provisioned for peak, the cheaper your batch gets.

**Q: Will running batch hurt my online (interactive) traffic?**
Harvesting is governed by an SLO guardrail you set per job (an interference-tolerance / price tier). Batch only fills the spare latency budget; when online traffic spikes, batch yields. In v0.7.0 this is a preview behind a feature flag; production-grade bounded-preemption hardening lands in a follow-up. You can also run batch on dedicated pools if you prefer zero colocation.

**Q: Are the results identical to running the model online?**
Yes — same model, same weights, same decoding parameters. Batch changes *scheduling and placement*, not *what is computed*. We ship an equivalence test in our benchmarks.

**Q: How do I migrate from OpenAI Batch?**
Change the OpenAI SDK `base_url` to your AIBrix gateway. Input/output JSONL and the batch lifecycle match. Bring your own model (vLLM-served) instead of a fixed provider catalog.

**Q: Which models / endpoints?**
Any model you serve on AIBrix (vLLM-based). Endpoints: chat/completions, completions, embeddings, rerank.

**Q: Where does my data go?**
Nowhere — it stays in your cluster and your object store (S3/TOS/GCS). That's the point of self-hosting: no third-party data egress, your compliance boundary.

**Q: What about completion-window guarantees?**
24-hour window like the closed APIs, with deadline-aware scheduling and an on-demand safety net (in spot-enabled releases) so deadlines are met even when harvested/spot capacity is scarce.

## A.3 Internal FAQ (stakeholders)

**Q: Why now? Why is this strategically worth it?**
Batch is the *ideal first vehicle* for AIBrix's data-plane innovations: its loose 24h SLO makes aggressive techniques (whole-job reordering, preemption, migration) *safe* in a way online serving never allows. It also gives us a sharp, quantifiable wedge vs closed providers — **cost** — and vs SkyPilot — **inference-native elasticity**. And most of the hard data-plane primitives already exist in AIBrix (prefix cache, KV offload, P/D disagg, routing, autoscaler); batch just needs to be connected to them.

**Q: Is the cost reduction real, or accounting?**
Both, transparently. Marginal-cost framing (harvested GPUs ≈ free) is the physical reason we can beat 50%-of-list. We *report* the conservative fully-amortized framing externally. See `03-experiments.md` §1 for the dual cost model. The claim is gated on the experiments — we publish no number we haven't measured against B0 (our own baseline) and B2 (OpenAI list).

**Q: What exactly is GA in v0.7.0 vs later?**
- **v0.7.0 (now):** hardened control plane (M0) + batch-aware scheduling first cut (M1) + online-fleet harvesting **preview/flag** (M2.a, single-model, iteration-level admission).
- **Later:** production bounded-preemption (Valve-style, M2.b), heterogeneous/offload cost tiers (M3), cross-region spot (M4, the SkyPilot bake-off), workflow batch (M5).

**Q: What's the biggest risk?**
Colocation touching online SLO — that's a paying-customer promise. Mitigations: ship harvesting behind a flag, default to a conservative tolerance, expose dedicated-pool mode, and gate GA on the W5 online-trace experiment showing p99 degradation within the set tier. Multi-model colocation remains an open problem we explicitly scope out of v0.7.0.

**Q: How do we position vs SkyPilot? Aren't we competing with a friend?**
We're complementary in framing but differentiated in capability. SkyPilot/SkyNomad are *workload-agnostic* VM orchestrators (whole-VM checkpoint, ~6-min cold start, gang preemption). AIBrix is *inference-engine-native*: checkpoint at request-queue + prefix-KV granularity (seconds), migrate disaggregated P/D units, and fuse cross-region spot **with online-fleet harvesting** — a two-level optimization neither tool does. The honest line: "SkyPilot runs your job anywhere cheaply; AIBrix runs your *inference* anywhere cheaply, and knows what a KV cache is." We can even run *on* SkyPilot-provisioned nodes.

**Q: What are we explicitly NOT doing in v0.7.0?**
Cross-cloud spot, CPU/attention offload, agentic workflow batch, multi-model colocation, and any "10×" marketing. Those are roadmap, not launch.

**Q: What does success look like 90 days post-launch?**
A published, reproducible "$/1M tokens vs OpenAI" curve (figure ① in `03-experiments.md`) with at least one credible operating point well under 50%-of-list; ≥N community deployments of harvesting; and the cost calculator updated to measured numbers.

## AIBrix Batch: Elastic, Cost-Optimizing Batch Inference for Your Own Fleet

AIBrix is a composable, cloud-native LLM inference infrastructure designed to deliver high performance and low cost at scale. With v0.7.0, we're extending that mission to **batch inference** — and rethinking what a batch API should be when *you* own the GPUs.

### Why we built this

Every batch API on the market makes the same trade: accept a 24-hour turnaround, get ~50% off the online price. It's a good deal — but it's a *pricing* deal on a black box. You can't bring your own hardware, can't see the cost structure, can't tune it, and can't use the most valuable asset you already have: **your online serving fleet, provisioned for peak and idle most of the day.**

We think the cheapest batch token is the one that runs on a GPU you've already paid for, during a trough. AIBrix Batch v0.7.0 is built to make that real — while staying OpenAI-Batch-compatible so adopting it is a base-URL change.

### What's new in v0.7.0

**1. Batch-aware scheduling (throughput → lower $/token).**
Because a batch job's full request set is known up front and the SLO is relaxed, AIBrix can optimize the *whole job* before it runs — building a global prefix tree so shared prompts are computed once instead of repeatedly recomputed under cache eviction, and ordering requests so compute-bound and memory-bound work balance on the GPU. This plugs directly into AIBrix's distributed prefix cache. On shared-prompt and mixed workloads we measure `[1.3–2×]` higher tokens/sec/GPU versus a vanilla offline engine. *(Figure: goodput vs baseline.)*

**2. Online-fleet harvesting — preview (elastic capacity → lower $/token).**
Batch requests can run as *fillers* in the spare latency budget of your online serving pods, admitted by a lightweight latency predictor and bounded by an SLO guardrail you set per job. When online traffic rises, batch yields. The result: batch capacity at close to the *marginal* cost of GPUs you already run. In trace-driven tests, online p99 latency degradation stays within `[5%]` while batch goodput fills the troughs. *(Figure: effective $/1M tokens vs harvested fraction, with the OpenAI Batch price line overlaid.)* This ships behind a feature flag in v0.7.0; dedicated-pool mode remains the default.

**3. A hardened, OpenAI-compatible control plane.**
Deadline-aware scheduling against the completion window, first-class cancellation, idempotent retries, per-job cost accounting (GPU/CPU-seconds, tokens, $), and Prometheus metrics for queue depth, goodput, JCT, and $/1M tokens.

**4. It builds on the AIBrix data plane you already know.**
KV-cache offloading, prefix caching, P/D disaggregation (StormService), and load-aware routing — batch now rides on these instead of spinning up an isolated vanilla engine.

### What's next

This is the first step of a longer roadmap to make AIBrix Batch the lowest-cost place to run batch inference *on infrastructure you control*:

- **Production-grade colocation** with bounded preemption latency and rate, so harvesting is safe under strict online SLOs.
- **Heterogeneous & offload cost tiers** — run batch on commodity GPUs and offload KV/attention to CPU, for cheaper-per-token large-model batch.
- **Cross-region / cross-cloud spot**, with *inference-native* checkpoint/restore at request-and-KV granularity (seconds, not minutes) — bringing the Sky Computing cost model to inference, the way only an inference-aware system can.
- **Workflow batch** — submit a batch of multi-step agentic workflows as a single optimized job.
